# Code of Conduct

## Our purpose

Encore exists to make sharing music effortless. That only works in a community
where people can contribute without fear of being embarrassed for asking a
question. This document states how we treat each other while doing that.

## Our standards

Behaviours we expect:

- Assuming the author meant well and asking before accusing.
- Respecting different experience levels; a "slow" question is often the
  documentation gap nobody else noticed.
- Giving specific, kind feedback, with a link instead of an opinion where
  possible.
- Accepting that scope is limited on purpose, and that "not now" is a valid
  answer.
- Crediting the people whose ideas, code or debugging you built on.

Unacceptable behaviour:

- Harassment, insults, personal or political attacks, sexualised language,
  attention-seeking behaviour in technical threads.
- Public or private shaming of contributors, including AI-assisted ones.
- Publishing someone's music library, network details or personal data.
- Deliberately wasting maintainers' time: drive-by demands, entitlement,
  dogpiling.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews or releases.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions, chats,
meetups, and in public representations of the project. It also applies to
behaviour outside these spaces when it affects safety inside them.

## Reporting

Report to the maintainers listed in [`.github/CODEOWNERS`](.github/CODEOWNERS)
by private security advisory or direct contact; do not open a public issue about
conduct. Include links to the affected discussions if you can.

Every report is reviewed by at least one maintainer who is not the subject of
the report. We keep the reporter's identity confidential. If you are not
comfortable contacting a maintainer directly, say so in the report and we will
find another route.

## Enforcement

1. **Correction** — a private, documented reminder; usually that is the end of
   it.
2. **Warning** — a public nudge and a documented condition to stop.
3. **Temporary ban** — no interaction for a stated period, chosen to fit the
   harm.
4. **Permanent ban** — for a pattern of violations or a single serious one.

Decisions are made by the maintainers, recorded privately, and can be revisited
with new information. Appeals are read.
